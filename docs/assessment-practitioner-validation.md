# Practitioner validation protocol

Status: **not yet conducted**. This protocol tests whether the synthetic workbench improves an investment assessment. It is not evidence of product effectiveness, client results or NIST compliance.

## Participants and safeguards

Invite three to five consulting delivery leads, transformation practitioners or business-case reviewers only after Beck authorises recruitment. Use fictional organisations and supplied synthetic records. Ask participants not to paste client names, operational records or confidential material. Explain that entered data stays in this browser but browser-local storage is not a confidentiality guarantee. Obtain consent before recording a session; notes alone are sufficient.

Use a separate test browser profile. Export a recovery backup before a session if it contains work worth retaining. Do not reset someone else's workspace. Note browser, device, viewport, release identifier and prior familiarity with the product.

## Research questions

1. Can a practitioner complete a small prepared assessment without developer help in approximately 30 minutes?
2. Can they identify the weakest material assumption, trace its source and explain how it could change the recommendation?
3. Can they compare AI against a credible process/rules option without treating released capacity as guaranteed cash savings?
4. Can they respond to a budget or adoption challenge and produce consistent deliverables without manually updating slides?
5. Can a second practitioner understand the exported recommendation without opening the app?

These are hypotheses. Record observed results, including failed tasks and assistance, rather than relabelling them as success.

## Session structure

Allow 45–60 minutes: five minutes of introduction, approximately 30 minutes of unaided assessment work, then a deliverable review and debrief. Give only the problem and task cards below; do not demonstrate the clicks first. Ask participants to think aloud. After two minutes without progress, ask what they expected; if you provide navigation help, mark the task as assisted and keep the clock running.

Use only two example journeys. Alternate which one is assigned first across participants. For a comparison with their existing spreadsheet/document workflow, use equivalent but not identical synthetic cases, counterbalance the order and record familiarity. A repeated case can introduce learning effects; disclose that limitation when reporting times.

### Journey A — Support operations investment assessment

Start from the Support Operations template. The scenario is a growing support queue, with disputed time-reduction estimates and a possible contractor-renewal avoidance mechanism. The values are synthetic assumptions, not measured outcomes.

Give these tasks in order:

1. State the client problem, proposed decision and decision deadline in your own words.
2. Compare rules-based improvement with human-reviewed AI assistance. Explain annual released hours, recurring cost and the difference between economic and cash-only value.
3. Identify one assumption whose evidence could materially change the recommendation. Record a specific evidence request with an owner and expected decision impact.
4. Reduce expected adoption by 20 percentage points in a saved what-if scenario. Explain what changed and what did not change in the underlying recommendation basis.
5. Decide whether to investigate, validate, recommend investment, defer or reject. Record a defensible recommendation with conditions and a next decision date. Missing evidence must not be quietly declared resolved to obtain a preferred label.
6. Produce a draft or reviewed deliverable, as appropriate, excluding internal notes. Explain the selected revision and limitations.

### Journey B — Reporting capacity, not an automatic cash saving

Start from the Reporting template. The scenario is repetitive report preparation. There is no stated headcount reduction or supplier-cost avoidance. The intended value is capacity for analysis.

1. Identify the non-AI alternative and explain why it remains in the comparison.
2. Explain why a positive economic result can coexist with weak or negative cash-only returns.
3. Add a synthetic evidence excerpt and review it with a rationale. Confirm that accepting the source does not automatically change a numeric assumption.
4. Set a validation hypothesis, measurable threshold, measurement method, accountable owner, budget ceiling and stop criterion.
5. Record an advisory recommendation. Change a material assumption and identify why the earlier snapshot now needs review without rewriting its historical contents.
6. Export the earlier reviewed snapshot and explain how its version differs from the current draft.

In Deliverables, use **Export basis** to select **Current draft / not reviewed** or a timestamped **Reviewed snapshot**. Keep **Include internal notes** unchecked for the client review. Compare the Investment brief preview with **Download Markdown**, **Download PowerPoint** (eight editable slides) and **Download Excel** (the full review workbook). Ask the participant to explain any stale-snapshot marker, incomplete/draft economics and references from summarized slides to the workbook. A reviewed recommendation can still have unknown economics; its label does not certify investment readiness. Confirm that an unsaved form edit is absent from the exports. Do not confuse these files with the full workspace recovery backup, which includes internal notes.

### Short blank-engagement check

If time permits, ask the participant to create a blank fictional engagement, choose GBP or EUR, add an opportunity and import a prepared synthetic baseline. Confirm they can find the mapping, period, units and source locator. Verify they understand that choosing another currency relabels values and does not perform currency conversion. Treat this as a separate task, not a reason to extend the 30-minute target silently.

## Observation record

Use participant codes, not personal details, in the results file.

| Field                             | Record                                                         |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Participant / relevant experience | Code and broad role only                                       |
| Release / browser / viewport      | Exact tested environment                                       |
| Case and order                    | Support, Reporting, optional blank                             |
| Task start / finish               | Elapsed time, including recovery                               |
| Outcome                           | Unassisted / assisted / failed / not attempted                 |
| Errors                            | Wrong input, misunderstanding, navigation or calculation issue |
| Recovery                          | What helped, and whether data was preserved                    |
| Rework                            | Re-entered values, manual slide edits or reconciliation        |
| Evidence understanding            | Source, uncertainty and decision impact explained?             |
| Value understanding               | Capacity distinguished from cash?                              |
| Deliverable clarity               | Correct revision, currency, basis and limitations identified?  |
| Quote / observation               | Consent-safe notes; no confidential information                |

For each task, record the participant's actual explanation. A correctly clicked button does not prove they understood the financial or evidence distinction.

## Independent deliverable review

Give an exported recommendation to another participant without app access or verbal explanation. Ask them to identify the decision, strongest alternative, annual hours, investment, recurring costs, economic versus cash result, weakest assumption and validation owner. Record omissions, contradictions and questions. Check whether excluded internal notes appear anywhere in the files.

## Reporting and release decisions

Report individual task outcomes and median/range of completion times; with three to five participants, do not claim statistical significance or generalise to all consulting teams. Separate novice learning from product defects and assisted from unassisted results. Compare like-for-like tasks when discussing improvement over spreadsheets.

Prioritise observed problems that change investment understanding, lose data, hide uncertainty or cause inconsistent exports. Resolve and retest those before presenting the prototype as useful for assessment work. Cosmetic preferences are secondary to correctness and comprehension.

Publish a short findings note containing the tested release, participant roles, method, observations, changes made and unresolved limitations. Until sessions actually occur, describe usability and time-saving claims as unvalidated hypotheses.
